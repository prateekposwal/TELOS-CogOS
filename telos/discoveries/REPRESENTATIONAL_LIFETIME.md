## Every Way of Thinking Has a Lifecycle

### 1. The Core Idea

Every *representation* — a theory, a model, a paradigm, a mathematical formalism, a cognitive frame — has a **lifecycle**. It is born, grows, plateaus, declines, and dies. But death is not deletion. Representations that die for the frontier remain *dormant* — still valid in bounded domains, still capable of revival when bridge conditions are met.

This is distinct from *theory failure* (a hypothesis falsified by evidence). Representational Death happens not because a representation is *wrong*, but because it is *exhausted* — it has stopped producing new predictions, novel explanations, or compression gains. It has reached its explanatory horizon.

### 3. Death ≠ Deletion

This is the critical distinction. Most architectures treat a failed theory as garbage-collected — deleted, forgotten, removed. Representational Lifetime says:

> **A representation dies at the frontier but lives in its domain.**

Newton is not *wrong*. Newton is *complete within[sub] bounded[sub]*. The representation's domain of validity is preserved. Dormancy is not deletion — it is *curation with revival metadata*.

A representation is dormant if:
- **Novelty rate** ≈ 0 (no new phenomena explained)
- **Prediction gain** < threshold (no improvement per unit evidence)
- **Residual growth** > 0 (unexplained variance is increasing)
- **Coverage** is stable (it explains what it explains, no more)

Revival happens when:
- **Bridge potential** detected: a new context or connection re-activates the representation
- The dormant representation explains something the active ones cannot

---

### 4. Measurable Metrics

```
Novelty Rate      N(t)  = |new_phenomena_explained| / Δt
Prediction Gain   P(t)  = Δ(accuracy) / Δ(evidence)
Residual Growth   R(t)  = unexplained_variance(t) - unexplained_variance(t-1)
Bridge Potential  B(r)  = max similarity(dormant_repr, active_problems)
Coverage          C(t)  = fraction of domain explained
```

A representation is **born** when N(t) > threshold.  
A representation **dies** when N(t) ≈ 0, P(t) ≈ 0, R(t) > 0 for K consecutive cycles.  
A representation is **dormant** if dead but C(t) > 0 in some bounded domain.  
A representation is **revived** when B(r) > threshold and a bridge is constructed.

### 5. Why This Matters for Cognitive Architectures

Most cognitive architectures lack a **meta-cognitive lifecycle manager**. They accumulate representations indefinitely, or they prune by recency, or they delete on falsification. None of these strategies capture the true utility curve of a representation.

A system with Representational Lifetime awareness can:
1. **Let go gracefully** — recognize when a representation's growth has plateaued
2. **Preserve domain utility** — keep Newton for engineering while retiring it for physics
3. **Maintain bridge potential** — tag dormant representations with revival conditions
4. **Avoid premature deletion** — Iwasawa wasn't wrong, it was waiting for Wiles
5. **Optimize meta-cognitive effort** — stop investing in declining representations; shift attention to frontier

**This is what TELOS calls a meta-cognitive primitive:** a structural pattern that operates *on* the system's own cognitive content, not just *in* it.
