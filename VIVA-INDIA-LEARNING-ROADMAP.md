## 1. Learning Roadmap Structure

### Phase 1: SEO Fundamentals (Weeks 1–2)

**Goal**: Understand how search engines work, learn core SEO vocabulary, and get comfortable with the foundational concepts.

| Week | Focus | Key Topics | Deliverable |
|------|-------|------------|-------------|
| 1 | How Search Works | Crawling, indexing, ranking; Google's E-E-A-T; SERP anatomy; search intent types (informational, navigational, transactional, commercial) | Personal notes + quiz score ≥ 80% on Google's "Fundamentals of Digital Marketing" SEO modules |
| 2 | On-Page & Off-Page Basics | Title tags, meta descriptions, H1–H3 structure, keyword placement, internal linking, backlink basics, local SEO intro | Annotated audit of vivaindia.com (identify 10 issues) |

**Milestone**: Can explain SEO to a client in 2 minutes without jargon.

---

### Phase 2: Technical SEO (Weeks 3–4)

**Goal**: Diagnose and fix the technical issues that prevent ranking.

| Week | Focus | Key Topics | Deliverable |
|------|-------|------------|-------------|
| 3 | Crawlability & Indexing | robots.txt, XML sitemaps, canonical tags, noindex/nofollow, crawl budget, log file analysis basics | Robots.txt + sitemap audit for all 3 domains |
| 4 | Performance & Structure | Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS), mobile-first indexing, HTTPS, structured data / Schema.org, hreflang for multi-language sites | Full technical SEO audit report using Screaming Frog (free 500 URLs) |

**Milestone**: Can run a Screaming Frog crawl, interpret the results, and prioritize fixes.

---

### Phase 3: Content Writing Mastery (Weeks 3–6)

**Goal**: Write content that ranks and converts — headlines, intros, body copy, CTAs.

| Week | Focus | Key Topics | Deliverable |
|------|-------|------------|-------------|
| 3 | Copywriting Frameworks | AIDA, PAS, Before–After–Bridge; headline formulas; hook writing; long-form vs. short-form | 5 headline variations for each of 3 VIVA India service pages |
| 4 | SEO Content Structure | Topic clusters, pillar pages vs. cluster content, content briefs, TF-IDF / NLP terms, search intent matching | Content brief template + 1 pillar page draft (2,000+ words) |
| 5 | Editing & Readability | Hemingway principles, Flesch–Kincaid, readability scoring, FAQ sections, featured snippet optimization | Edited version of the pillar page scoring ≤ Grade 8 readability |
| 6 | Content Calendar & Strategy | Editorial calendars, content gap analysis, competitor content audits, repurposing workflows | 90-day content calendar for vivaindia.com.mx |

**Milestone**: Can independently produce a publish-ready 2,000-word SEO blog post in under 4 hours.

### Phase 4: Spanish Content Creation (Weeks 5–8)

**Goal**: Produce native-quality Spanish SEO content.

| Week | Focus | Key Topics | Deliverable |
|------|-------|------------|-------------|
| 5 | Spanish SEO Fundamentals | Spanish keyword research (Google.mx, Google.co), LATAM search behavior differences, Spanish SERP features | Keyword list: 50 primary + 200 long-tail terms across 3 markets |

**Milestone**: Can produce a 1,500-word Spanish blog post that reads native, with proper regional variants, in under 3 hours.

## 6. Practical Exercises

### Exercise 1: SEO Audit Practice (Week 1–2)

**Setup**: Use vivaindia.com (or any site you have access to)

**Steps**:
1. Run Screaming Frog crawl (free up to 500 URLs). Export the full report.
2. Check for:
   - Pages with missing or duplicate title tags
   - Missing meta descriptions
   - Missing H1 tags or multiple H1s
   - Images without alt text
   - 4xx and 5xx errors
   - Redirect chains (301 → 301)
   - Pages with thin content (< 300 words)
3. Open Google Search Console. Check:
   - Index coverage (how many pages are indexed vs. submitted)
   - Any coverage errors or warnings
   - Mobile usability issues
4. Run PageSpeed Insights on 5 key pages. Note LCP, INP, CLS scores.
5. Write up findings in a structured report (use this template):

```markdown
# SEO Audit Report — vivaindia.com
Date: [date]
Auditor: [name]

## Executive Summary
[2-3 sentences: overall health score, top 3 issues]

## Technical Issues
| Issue | Pages Affected | Severity | Fix Priority |
|-------|---------------|----------|-------------|
| ... | ... | ... | ... |

## Content Issues
[...]

## Quick Wins (can fix today)
[...]

## Long-term Fixes (need planning)
[...]
```

**Time**: 3–4 hours per domain

## 7. Spanish Language Resources

### Spanish Writing Tools & Databases

| Tool | Purpose | Free? | Key Feature |
|------|---------|-------|-------------|
| **SpanishDict** (spanishdict.com) | Complete Spanish reference — dictionary, conjugation, grammar | Free | Contextual examples, pronunciation audio |
| **RAE** (dle.rae.es) | Royal Spanish Academy dictionary — authoritative Spanish | Free | Definitive source for word meanings and usage |
| **WordReference** | Spanish-English dictionary with forum discussions on nuance | Free | Forum discussions explain subtle differences |
| **DeepL Translator** | High-quality machine translation | Free (limited) | Better than Google for context and tone |
| **LanguageTool** | Spanish grammar and style checker | Free (limited) | Catches grammar errors that spellcheck misses |
| **Linguee** | Bilingual corpus showing how terms are used in professional contexts | Free | Great for formal/business Spanish |

### Translation Quality Assurance Methods

**The 5-Step Translation QA Process**:

1. **AI Draft**: Use DeepL (preferred) or ChatGPT to generate initial Spanish draft
2. **Grammar Pass**: Run through LanguageTool to catch basic errors
3. **Cultural Review**: Check for:
   - Region-specific vocabulary (Mexico: "computadora" vs. Spain: "ordenador")
   - Formality level appropriate for the audience
   - Idioms that don't translate literally
   - Currency references (MXN pesos, COP pesos — clarify which)
   - Date formats (DD/MM/YYYY for LATAM)
4. **Readability Check**: Paste into Hemingway Editor (Spanish works in Hemingway)
5. **Native Review**: Post key phrases on HiNative or have a native speaker review

### How to Write Spanish SEO Content Without Being Native

**Core Principles**:

1. **Do keyword research IN Spanish**: Never translate English keywords to Spanish. Use Google Keyword Planner with Spanish-language settings. Search volumes differ — "viajar" vs. "viajar a" vs. "hacer un viaje" are different markets.

2. **Study competitor content**: Read the top 10 Google results for your target keyword. Note:
   - Vocabulary they use
   - Sentence structure
   - Regional variations
   - Content length
   - Types of subheadings

3. **Use Spanish structural templates**: Spanish content tends to be:
   - More elaborate in introductions (less "get to the point" than English)
   - More formal for professional services
   - More personal and narrative for travel content

4. **Regional targeting matters**:
   - **Mexico (vivaindia.com.mx)**: Use "usted" for formal, "tú" for friendly; Mexican Spanish vocabulary
   - **Colombia (vivaindia.com.co)**: Use "usted" (common in Colombian professional context), "vos" for friendly (depending on region)
   - **General LATAM (vivaindia.com)**: Use "usted" as safe default; neutral vocabulary; avoid country-specific slang

5. **Never use Google Translate as final copy**: It produces "neutral" Spanish that reads awkwardly to any native speaker. Use it as a starting point only.

6. **Build a glossary**: Create a living document of:
   - Standardized terms for VIVA India (e.g., "paquete turístico" vs. "tour" vs. "experiencia de viaje")
   - Terms to avoid (false friends, calques from English)
   - Region-specific terms for each market

### When to Use AI Translation vs. Human Writing

| Scenario | Method | Why |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Blog post (informational) | AI draft → heavy human edit | AI captures structure; human adds voice, cultural nuance |
| Service/landing page | Human writing with AI assistance | Conversion copy needs human persuasion and cultural fit |
| FAQ section | AI draft → human review | Structured format works well for AI |
| Email marketing | Human writing | Personal tone required |
| Meta descriptions | AI draft → human polish at scale | Short format, formulaic — AI is efficient |
| Schema markup | AI-generated (structured data) | Schema is machine-readable — AI is fine |
| Social media | Human writing with AI ideas | Brand voice needs consistency |

### Grammar & Style Guides for Spanish

1. **"El buen uso del español en internet"** — Real Academia Española guidelines for digital Spanish
2. **AP Stylebook (Spanish edition)** — Writing standards for professional Spanish content
3. **Manual de estilo del español para medios de comunicación** — Style guide for media/communication Spanish
4. **Create your own house style guide** (see Exercise 2 output) — Once you've decided on conventions, document them for consistency

**Rules for AI Use**:

3. Verify all facts, dates, and claims — AI hallucinates
4. Check for AI "tells" (overly formal tone, repetitive structure, generic phrases)
5. Use AI as a starting point, not the final product
